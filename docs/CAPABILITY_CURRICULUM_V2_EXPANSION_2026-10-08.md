# Capability Curriculum v2 Expansion

Status: Founder approved direction
Approval date: 8 October 2026
Scope: First planned post-v1 Capability curriculum expansion

## Decision

Response Integrity will add a new System Intelligence Deep Dive:

**Why Training Continues Beyond Clarity**

This is a deliberate Capability Curriculum v2 expansion. It must not alter, invalidate, or silently widen the active Curriculum v1 proof while v1 is still closing through OS Delayed Retrieval and OS Interleaved Transfer.

Curriculum v1 remains frozen until its current non-review lifecycle proof is complete.

## Why this Deep Dive exists

A Specialist can understand how Clarity, Structured Execution, Controlled Discomfort, and Time Pressure Stability operate while still holding a weak underlying model of why the later phases exist.

The critical failure mode is:

> If the student already understands the maths, why are we still training them?

A Specialist who cannot answer that question may mechanically follow RI while privately treating later-phase work as redundant, arbitrary difficulty, unnecessary withholding of help, or timing for its own sake.

RI requires Specialists to understand and be able to reason from the purpose of the operating model, not merely comply with it.

## Governing distinction

The central teaching statement is:

**Diagnosis finds the correct load. Training changes the response under that load.**

This derives directly from the approved Training Evidence and Stability Contract:

- Diagnosis discovers the earliest unsupported response layer using the minimum evidence needed to resolve placement.
- Training deliberately creates repeated exposure in order to change the response.
- Diagnosis is evidence-complete, not rep-complete.
- Training is exposure-complete and evidence-authorized.

Finding where performance stops holding is not the same thing as changing the response at that layer.

## Phase meaning

The Deep Dive must teach the four phases as four different capability transformations.

### Clarity

Clarity changes how clearly the student sees and understands what to do.

Core question:

> Can the student recognize the problem, understand the method, and explain why it applies?

### Structured Execution

Structured Execution changes how reliably the student executes what they already understand.

Core question:

> Can the student carry out what they know independently, in order, and repeatably?

### Controlled Discomfort

Controlled Discomfort changes the student's response when difficulty and uncertainty are introduced.

Core question:

> Can the student keep using what they know when the problem becomes difficult, unfamiliar-feeling, or not immediately obvious?

The point is not to reteach the academic method when the method is already intact.

The intervention is the deliberate progression of exposure:

`Controlled Entry -> No Rescue -> Repeat Exposure`

The target is controlled entry, tolerance, recovery, lower rescue dependence, and repeatable use of the existing method under difficulty.

### Time Pressure Stability

Time Pressure Stability changes whether the trained response remains intact under real urgency.

Core question:

> Can the student preserve start, structure, pace control, and completion integrity when time pressure is added?

The intervention is the deliberate progression:

`Structure Under Timer -> Repeated Timed Execution -> Full Constraint`

The timer is not merely measurement. The individualized pressure condition is part of the training load.

## Why RI does not stop at Clarity

Academic understanding is necessary, but it does not guarantee usable performance.

A student can know what to do and still fail because:

- they cannot execute independently;
- difficulty destabilizes their response;
- they become rescue-dependent;
- they abandon structure when unsure;
- urgency changes their decisions;
- pace breaks completion integrity.

These are not additional academic knowledge gaps. They are conditions under which existing knowledge stops becoming reliable performance.

RI therefore trains more than understanding.

The operating progression is:

**understanding -> execution -> difficulty stability -> urgency stability**

## Diagnosis versus Training

The Deep Dive must make this contrast explicit.

Diagnosis asks:

> Where does the response stop holding?

Training asks:

> Can we make the response hold there repeatedly?

Diagnosis may need only enough clean evidence to resolve the placement question.

Training may deliberately repeat the same condition because the repeated exposure is part of the intervention.

A successful isolated rep after Diagnosis does not prove that the response has changed durably.

## Load-preservation mental model

The Deep Dive should give Specialists one reusable mental model:

**Each RI phase preserves what the earlier phase established while adding one new load.**

- Clarity: Can you see what to do?
- Structured Execution: Can you do it yourself?
- Controlled Discomfort: Can you still do it when it becomes difficult?
- Time Pressure Stability: Can you still do it when time pressure is real?

If removing the added load restores the earlier capability, RI has identified something specific about what must be trained.

## Placement in Curriculum v2

Module: **System Intelligence**

Planned order:

1. How to Diagnose
2. **Why Training Continues Beyond Clarity**
3. How to Interpret Prompts
4. How Baselines Are Established
5. How the System Resolves Uncertainty

This placement is intentional:

- How to Diagnose teaches how RI finds the unsupported layer.
- Why Training Continues Beyond Clarity teaches what RI is changing once that layer is found.
- The remaining System Intelligence Deep Dives teach how to operate the evidence system correctly.

## Curriculum-version rule

This Deep Dive must not be inserted into active Curriculum v1.

Curriculum v1 remains:

- 15 post-Sandbox Masteries;
- 24-hour spacing;
- Operating System Delayed Retrieval v1;
- Operating System Interleaved Transfer v1.

Curriculum v2 will add this Deep Dive as the sixteenth post-Sandbox Deep Dive.

That expansion requires:

1. a new Deep Dive lesson;
2. a 45-item Mastery bank with a 15-question live form;
3. Founder review of the lesson and Mastery bank;
4. an updated Capability blueprint and System Intelligence module definition;
5. a new cumulative OS Retrieval bank covering all 16 post-Sandbox Deep Dives;
6. a new cumulative OS Transfer bank covering all 16 post-Sandbox Deep Dives;
7. explicit Curriculum v2 sequencing and qualification authority;
8. a migration/currentness policy for Specialists who already qualified under Curriculum v1.

Existing valid v1 evidence must remain historically valid. New knowledge creates a new curriculum version; it does not rewrite old evidence.

## Authoring standard

The lesson must be naturally readable and learnable.

It must:

- explain reasons before enforcing rules;
- use concrete student examples;
- distinguish academic teaching from response conditioning;
- show why CD and TPS are legitimate training even when no new mathematics is being taught;
- explain the developmental purpose of support boundaries and repeated exposure;
- avoid presenting Specialists as passive rule-followers;
- give Specialists enough understanding to defend the operating model in their own reasoning without inventing doctrine;
- preserve the approved phase contracts and evidence rules exactly.

## Approval boundary

Founder approval here covers:

- the need for this Deep Dive;
- its conceptual purpose;
- its System Intelligence placement;
- the governing distinction between Diagnosis and Training;
- the four-phase explanatory model;
- treatment as a Curriculum v2 expansion rather than a mutation of v1.

It does **not** yet approve:

- final slide copy;
- final lesson sequence;
- Mastery questions/options/feedback;
- cumulative v2 Retrieval/Transfer banks;
- migration rules for already-qualified Specialists.

Those remain separate authored artifacts and require their own review.
