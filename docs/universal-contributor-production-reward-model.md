# Universal Contributor Production Model

## Core Realization

The platform does not reward roles.

It rewards contributors.

A contributor is anything that can own a unique production link and produce measurable opportunity.

Examples:

- a person
- an Instagram campaign
- a Facebook campaign
- a TikTok account
- a school partnership
- a parent referral network
- an affiliate
- a community organization
- a marketing creative
- a QR code on a flyer
- a WhatsApp group
- a specialist recruitment campaign

If it can produce opportunity, it can be a contributor.

---

## Universal Production Reward Rule

A Production Reward is unlocked only when this chain is verified:

```text
Opportunity
    ↓
Capture
    ↓
Qualification
    ↓
Trial
    ↓
Subscription

✓ Conversion Verified

↓

Reward Triggered
```

No subscription.

No reward.

---

## Student Production

Each contributor owns a unique tracking link.

Example:

```text
Instagram Campaign

Owner:
Sarah

Link:
ri.co.za/i/sarah-back-to-school
```

All parents entering through that link are attributed to Sarah's campaign.

Pipeline example:

```text
Instagram Campaign

↓

12 Leads

↓

8 Qualified

↓

5 Trials

↓

4 Subscriptions
```

Reward example:

```text
4 × R100 = R400
```

---

## Shared Ownership

Two contributors can jointly own one campaign.

Example:

```text
Back-to-School Campaign

Owners:
Sarah
John
```

Conversions can be split by predefined ownership rules.

Example split:

```text
20 total subscriptions

↓

10 attributed to Sarah
10 attributed to John
```

Each earns:

```text
10 × R100
```

The split is defined at campaign creation and handled automatically by the platform.

---

## Organic Traffic

Organic traffic has no contributor owner.

Example:

```text
Google Search
↓
Website
↓
Subscription
```

No producer is attached.

Therefore:

```text
Platform retains full R250.
```

No Production Reward is created.

---

## Specialist Production (Separate Pipeline)

Specialist production does not optimize for subscriptions.

It optimizes for capacity.

Pipeline:

```text
Recruitment Campaign

↓

Opportunity

↓

Application

↓

Training

↓

Sandbox

↓

Trial-Ready

↓

Certified Live Specialist
```

Each applicant carries contributor lineage from source to certification state.

Example:

```text
University Campaign

↓

25 Applications

↓

15 Training

↓

12 Sandbox

↓

8 Trial-Ready

↓

6 Certified Live Specialists
```

MVP rule:

- no specialist Production Rewards yet
- specialist pipeline is tracked for capacity analytics

Future option:

If specialist acquisition becomes a bottleneck, specialist rewards can be added without changing the architecture.

## Unified Architecture

Everything is a contributor.

```text
Contributor

↓

Owns Link

↓

Produces Opportunities

↓

Pipeline

↓

Conversion

↓

Production Reward (if eligible)
```

Different contributor types use different pipeline stages, but the same tracking and attribution engine.

## Why This Model Scales

This removes the need for separate reward systems for:

- affiliates
- referral partners
- school partnerships
- influencers
- social media managers
- marketing campaigns
- QR codes
- community ambassadors

They are all contributors with production links.

What changes by contributor type:

- what they produce (students or specialists)
- which pipeline stages apply
- whether verified conversion currently unlocks a Production Reward

What stays universal:

- tracking
- lineage
- ownership
- attribution
- progression
- verification logic

This is an elegant MVP architecture because one production engine can scale in multiple directions without creating separate commission systems for every channel.
